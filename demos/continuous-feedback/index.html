Composition-operator stabilization

From a local section to a feedback law

A local right inverse separates one hard question into two concrete objects: an associated state field whose stability can be checked directly, and a feedback law that makes the original plant reproduce that field.

The factorization

The section recovers each requested velocity.

A polynomial example

The controller replaces one unstable velocity component.

The example below is local: all initial states lie near the equilibrium at the origin. The middle and right trajectories coincide because the feedback makes the original system equal the associated stable field.

Original plant ẋ = f(x,u) = x12 + x22 + x2 x1x2 + x22 + u3

02 · Associated state field

A stable second component is chosen while retaining the plant's first component.

03 · Original plant + feedback

f(x,u(x)) = g(x)

The feedback correction, shown in green, converts the native velocity into the chosen stable velocity.

native velocity control correction resultant
Choose the stable second velocity g2(x) = −½x1 − 2x2
Make the original plant reproduce it x1x2 + x22 + u(x)3 = −½x1 − 2x2
The continuous feedback u(x) = ∛−½x1 − 2x2 − (x1x2 + x22)

The Jacobian of g at the origin has eigenvalues (−2 ± √2)/2, both negative. Thus the displayed associated system—and therefore the controlled plant—is locally exponentially stable.
