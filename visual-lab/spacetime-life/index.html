How to read the stack. The bottom layer is the oldest generation currently visible; at first, it is the grid you drew. Conway’s B3/S23 rule produces each next layer: a dead cell is born with exactly three living neighbors, while a living cell survives with two or three. When the selected history window is full, the oldest layer falls away but the generation count continues. A still life forms straight pillars, an oscillator forms a repeating braid, and a glider leaves a diagonal trail because it moves through space as time rises.
