How to read the plots
Parameter sweep
Each frame recomputes the trajectory from the same starting point at a fixed r. The sweep steps through a family of systems.
Trajectory separation
The nearby trajectory starts at the same point with one coordinate changed. The distance plot compares the two trajectories at equal times. Growth over a finite interval illustrates how the trajectories separate in this run.
Plane crossings
The vertical plane has horizontal normal (cos α, sin α, 0) and signed offset d. Its horizontal coordinate is u = −x sin α + y cos α. The section records the original 3D trajectory crossing in the chosen direction, after discarding an initial transient. The Hopf orbit meets a transverse section through the origin once per direction per revolution.
Orbit peaks
The Lorenz sweep samples local maxima of z after a transient, using one fixed starting point for each parameter. It can miss coexisting attractors and unstable cycles. A square marks numerical convergence near a sink. The Hopf sweep shows the exact limiting x peak for the preset starting point. The return plot pairs consecutive numerical peaks of the current trajectory.
Cubic coefficients
For Lorenz, the characteristic polynomial is λ3 + a1λ2 + a2λ + a3, with a1 = σ + 1 + b fixed. The shaded sink region has a2 > 0, a3 > 0, and a3 < a1a2. At the boundary a3 = a1a2 with a2 > 0, a conjugate pair is purely imaginary. The two nonzero Lorenz equilibria share the same coefficients by symmetry.
