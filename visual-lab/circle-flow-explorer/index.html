Mathematical notes

Equilibria and topology. An equilibrium is an angle where f(θ*) = 0. Simple equilibria around a circle alternate stable and unstable. They can therefore be created or destroyed only in pairs. With no equilibria, every trajectory keeps circling in the same direction.

Rotation and mean frequency. If f never vanishes, one turn takes T = ∫₀²π dθ / |f(θ)|, and the signed mean angular frequency is Ω = ±2π/T. Uneven speed around the circle is therefore summarized by a single long-term rotation rate.

The locking threshold. In θ̇ = ω − a sin θ, equilibria exist when |ω| ≤ |a|. At equality, a stable–unstable pair collides. Just beyond the collision the orbit rotates, but lingers near the vanished pair: the infinite-period bottleneck.

The trajectory tube. The tube is time × S¹. A solution t ↦ θ(t) is a curve on its surface. Rotation winds around the tube, while phase locking approaches a longitudinal equilibrium line. When a parameter is animated, the tube records the resulting time-dependent motion.

Rolling the bifurcation diagram. The plane plots each equilibrium angle against one chosen parameter while holding the others fixed; its top and bottom edges represent the same circle seam 0 = 2π. Rolling the plane joins those edges into a cylinder. The gold slice is the parameter value used by the other three views.

Transcritical exchange. In the transcritical example, θ* = π is fixed while θ* = π + μ moves. At μ = 0 the branches cross and exchange stability rather than being created or destroyed. With x = θ − π, the vector field begins as ẋ = ½x(μ − x) + higher-order terms, the transcritical normal form.

Pitchforks and symmetry. In the symmetric pitchfork example, ε = 0 enforces a reflection symmetry. At μ = 0, one equilibrium loses stability while two stable equilibria emerge. A nonzero ε breaks the symmetry and unfolds the pitchfork into ordinary saddle-nodes.
