04 · Memory in a scalar flow

Hysteresis: the path depends on the past

Ready to run a parameter loop

Increasing r Decreasing r

In the bistable interval, the same parameter value supports two stable equilibria separated by one unstable equilibrium. As r increases, the tracked state stays near the lower branch and tips upward after the right fold; as r decreases, it stays near the upper branch and tips downward after the left fold. The static folds are the quasi-static thresholds. At finite sweep speed, tipping can lag beyond them; the speed control changes that lag and the recorded loop width.

Mathematical notes

Random analytic families. Each seeded family combines two or three separated analytic event blocks chosen from saddle-node, transcritical, and pitchfork forms. Positive trigonometric and logarithmic factors vary the flow speed without changing the equilibria. This generator is deliberately curated rather than statistically generic: exact transcritical and pitchfork events require special structure, but including them makes the generated diagrams more useful for comparison.

N-branch crossings and pitchfork unfoldings. The adjustable n-branch family (including n = 4) is a genuine analytic scalar differential equation with several equilibrium branches meeting at one parameter value. These deliberately constructed crossings are high-codimension events, not structurally stable one-parameter bifurcations. A small generic perturbation separates them into simpler events. The pitchfork panel shows the complete local cubic unfolding α + βx² + εrx + σx³: perfect, transcritical-plus-saddle-node, three-saddle-node, triple-root-boundary, and one-saddle-node regimes. This is locally exhaustive near an ordinary cubic pitchfork, not a classification of higher-order or global degeneracies.
